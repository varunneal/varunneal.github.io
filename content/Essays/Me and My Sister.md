In various times in my life, struggling with This question of a Girl or That question of a Diet, or my ultimate fate on this planet, I try to turn to what my ancestor may have done. I'm not entirely sure why. Is it because I share their genetics, and so their physiology, and am similar in appearances and build? Or is it spiritual, and I believe they are ever-watching me, and will give me boons if I live in a way they find righteous?

I think physiology plays a big role in what one finds pleasant or intolerable. Me and my sister share a common interest in drugs but have rather low tolerances to do them. I think this is a good trait to have. We've not really been able to get addicted to anything. We've both smoked plenty of weed in the past, but both of us are incapable of becoming stoners. We've agreed that smoking even a few times in a week causes us to feel some persistent haze, tiredness, and repulsion from trying again. Even cigarettes, which are supposed to be rather addictive, have had little hold on me. At one point—in a deep depression in my life...— I was trying over five a day. I still smoke them sometimes, but my body has "turned away" and usually only a couple a week is the most I can handle. Another time, while experimenting with microdosing mushrooms I fell ill (& caught covid for the first time) and I haven't touched them since. I've done acid a few many times but at some point I took it twice in a month and the utter depletion I felt in my body and brain near the end of the trip convinced me I should take a break... and a long break it has been, coming on two years now. Nearly every time I've done cocaine I've caught a cold after. In short, my body is sensitive... 

I imagine certain ancestors watch me sick for days after every bender and say "of course! don't you know we weren't in the habit of such things?!" All my ancestors in living memory are sober, serious people, & protective of their mental faucilties. My mom said her father used to smoke a cigarette once a month, "but not inhale the smoke". I'd like to imagine in my distant line I had a certain forefather surrounded with the various plant medicines of ancient India, and with slight inclinations away from that world, his progeny is now not easily the substance-using type.

I think my body is quite sensitive in matters of the heart, too. I recently discovered scientific proof of this fact on myself. You see, in palmology, a certain *Ring of Venus* appears for some people at the base of the middle and ring fingers. I happen to have this *Ring* and on both hands; it forms a near perfect semicircle with a small gap in the middle. The apparent interpretation of such a symbol is difficulty to its wearer in matters in love... and also a slowness in getting over loss: heartbreak, and death. 

As such, having felt and felt quite strongly such losses in my life. Each loss hurts me, and I feel reluctant now to "get into things". Coming off last winter, where I lost so much, I feel that losing anymore could kill me. Heartbreak seems to have this effect on me! 
But it must be physiological. Because I see friend move from lover to lover in weeks. What a strong constitution he must have! *Que impresivo*, that some have the ability to "have a roster", to be with many at once. Their hearts must be so strong, these healthy people. 
I owe my weakness also, I think, to probably a long strain of domestic monagamy present in my family for perhaps hundreds of generations. I do not think I am confined to such (certainly my sister isn't...), but it is something like what is most natural to me.

My sister, as I've hinted, is more daring than I at matters of love. She has a girlfriend, reads bell hooks, and has entertained much that I think I'd be unable to in matters of love... I contemplate much about queerness in my life. She is bi. A first cousin my age who I grew up with quite closely is trans. Another couple close cousins are definitely *something* queer as well... I don't know if this is genetic, in some way. Was it the natural emergence of a suppressed familial undercurrent, that found the right generation where it could express itself? Or is it just that we all grew up in California, and the liberalness of our parents and neighboring society? Or is a total coincedence entirely..? I'm not sure. The tether is so loose, for us in the Second Generation. Our parents generation (the First) was the first to date, to experience modern romance. They were still much following certain customs and rules. My sister and cousins have let it go entirely. What bravery this takes! My mind wants the security of generational wisdom, the telos for love. But I also feel there is nothing to hold onto. I think that the West, who developed this project, had many generations to acclimitize to this world of freedoms. At least I can copy their notes... have you read Bertrand Russell's *Marriage and Morals*?

I wonder if my ancestors were interested in music. My mother's mother sang quite well, and in her younger days, it is said, she had the voice to be a star. I remember her singing but she was much older and with little practice, but still her high and powerful voice could sing those old Bollywood songs. 

My mother says as a kid she would go over to her landlord's house because they had a piano. She would plod acoustically on keys. I think her first time really being with an instrument. My parents both much enjoyed music, listening to much 80s hits on casettes and radios. I think my father has a natural knack for playing, in college picking up the (hawaiian) guitar and keyboard. But I also think that such musical abilities were deeply suppressed for many generations, allowing their full rise of expression in me and my sister. 

Well, perhaps my sister and I are not so talented as to hold claim to the final musical expression of our familial line. But we love music. I hummed in class and annoyed teachers and when I learned to whistle I annoyed them much more. Tapping on my desk I'd create a little symphony. 
My sister was controlling of the radio and the aux on car rides and would chastise me if the songs I liked were insufficiently interesting. In middle school she much liked coldplay and then radiohead.  When I began to come into my own music taste in middle school, we could bond much over shared tracks, and I would try hard to impress her with my finds. In college, I joined a band and for the first time became obsessed with genres besides rap: indie folk, shoegaze, emo, techno, ambient, noise. What a world had opened up to me! Unfortunately, by this time, my sister got really into house music, a genre I find kinda shitty, and that's also her preference for DJing. I go to her shows sometimes, and enjoy techno sets that usually come on a little later at night.

I find it funny to summarize us aptly as: My sister is a model slash DJ in Brooklyn and I'm a coder in Manhattan. Both characterizations, I think, betray our depth. But I secretly find that even funnier. I find my job nauseatingly normal. I also think it is remarkably similar to what my ancestors were up to. My people, *Kayastha*, were clerical people. "Librarians". Bookkeepers, historians, scribes. I think this is the closest pre-modern job to a software engineer. It makes sense for me! My sister possesses many opposite aspects of our lineage. She's always had a sort of natural genius for maps, names, new languages. Apaprently she became fluent in Spanish in about two years in high school—I took four to be *alright*. She enjoys deep dives into cultural movements she resonates with (like, the birth of house music) and she has the ability to write well about what she learns. I think she is incapable of having a desk job, but she has plenty the talent. 

She is a professional model. She is tall—four full inches more than me—and athletically built. If my sister was only my height (still tall for a girl!) I think I would be a lot more resentful of my stature. But given that she is so very tall, and graced with such obvious beauty, I find the situation absurd enough to be charming. It took me quite a while in life to discover I had any athletic abilities whatsoever. Most of my adolescense I was pretty pudgy. In high school, I learned I love to boulder, and both calisthenics and weightlifting were fun, and I could put on muscle faster than I expected. 
 
I think my sister and I are both naturally spiritual. She told me of this mushroom trip she took in a storm on the beach, where she realized she would return at death to the ocean, and how she no longer possessed fear of death. I internalize religion more like my mother. I enjoy rituals and prayers, incense and statues. 

I think we understand each other. We used to fight a lot as kids. But before that, I remember, we used to play.
