Epistemological dualism—a rule of reality, that we cannot definitively prove whether the material or consciousness-first worldview is correct using scientific methods. The very nature of scientific inquiry ensures that any evidence—especially for phenomena like telepathy—remains necessarily ambiguous. This is why the [Telepathy Tapes](https://thetelepathytapes.com/), which I deeply want to believe in (and I *do* believe that *I* do believe in) and all similar accounts, narrowly lack conclusive proof. The evidence bends to the interpretative lens of the observer, shaped by their psychological state.

The psyche itself acts as the filter through which reality is perceived. Some individuals are elevated to a state where they see meaning and interconnectedness, while others remain in a haze, incapable of grasping deeper implications. Even those who directly experience the inexplicable—like the mothers in the tapes—may harbor epistemic uncertainty. Their own doubts, self-perceived ignorance, or even momentary hallucinations could cause them to question themselves, dampening the transformative impact of their experiences. Some people move through life so numbed that even an encounter with the divine would provoke only fleeting awe before dissolving into the mundane.

The advent of science—particularly statistical methods, p-values, and falsifiability—created the framework by which "[[Magic]]" could be dismissed. It didn’t necessarily disprove it but established criteria that made it easy to wave away. By inventing the means to measure reality in a way that excluded the immeasurable, science didn't just describe the world—it forced us to discard one half.
