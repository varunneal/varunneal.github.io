# (1) the tapes
Epistemological dualism—a rule of reality, that we cannot definitively prove whether the material or consciousness-first worldview is correct using scientific methods. The very nature of scientific inquiry ensures that any evidence—especially for phenomena like telepathy—remains necessarily ambiguous. This is why the [Telepathy Tapes](https://thetelepathytapes.com/), which I deeply want to believe in (and I *do* believe that *I* do believe in) and all similar accounts, narrowly lack conclusive proof. The evidence bends to the interpretative lens of the observer, shaped by their psychological state.

The technique used in the Tapes that allows non-verbal autists to communciate is called Facilitated Communication (FC). Basically, an assistant (usually the mother of the autistic child) will hold the arm of the kid while the kid taps or points to letters. "Spelling". 

What's the controversy? The idea is that the mother is typically manipulating or cueing the kid to spell what she wants. Here's a seemingly-damning piece of evidence [critics of FC](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wB3ZbWdBrVo) point to as the whole thing being bullshit:

>Message passing tests come in a few varieties, but in a prototypical case, the autistic person is shown one picture and the person assisting them is shown a different one. In studies using this test, the autistic person was usually (but not always) unable to report what they had seen (and sometimes they reported what the assistant had seen, demonstrating that the assistant can influence the person they are assisting).^[1]


The linked video above shows this in action. The mother is shown a picture of shoes, the child is shown a cat, and "s-n-e-a-k-e-r" is spelled. Asbolutely damning! Clearly the mother is the one doing all the actual spelling. But if believe in the Telepathy Tapes, you might see this as a powerful piece of evidence for the supernaturality of the kid. Why? He chose to spell what his mom saw, because he can read her mind! 

The Tapes claim that these autistic kids are not particularly embodied. They are unable to control their limbs well, and use their mother's body as a sort of bridge to the natural world. One kids described himself as being mostly unaware that he even had arms and legs. Such behavior seems consistent with the often-flailing, uncoordinated movements of these non-speakers. Anyway, the kids who purportedly experience this telepathic link with their mother are claimed to be able to see through her eyes, or even through a set of eyes ambiently aware but not quite embodied, similar to [remote viewing](https://www.amazon.com/PSI-Spies-Jim-Marrs/dp/1588790231). If this is the case, and the pure-hearted kids are unfamiliar with the deceptive nature of double blind setup, why shouldn't they spell what their mother sees...?

[1] (from the [Jaswal Lab](https://jaswallab.org/summary-and-faqs/), which claims to have rigorously proved FC as true via eye tracking.)

# (2) the psyche
The psyche itself acts as the filter through which reality is perceived. Some individuals are elevated to a state where they see meaning and interconnectedness, while others remain in a haze, incapable of grasping deeper implications. Even those who directly experience the inexplicable—like the mothers in the tapes—may harbor epistemic uncertainty. Their own doubts, self-perceived ignorance, or even momentary hallucinations could cause them to question themselves, dampening the transformative impact of their experiences. Some people move through life so numbed that even an encounter with the divine would provoke only fleeting awe before dissolving into the mundane.

# (3) p-values
The advent of science—particularly statistical methods, p-values, and falsifiability—created the framework by which "[[Magic]]" could be dismissed. It didn’t necessarily disprove it but established criteria that made it easy to wave away. By inventing the means to measure reality in a way that excluded the immeasurable, science didn't just describe the world—it forced us to discard one half.
